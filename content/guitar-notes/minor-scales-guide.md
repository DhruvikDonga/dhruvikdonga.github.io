If the Major Scale is a sunny morning walking through fields, the **Natural Minor Scale** is a quiet drive on a rainy night. It is the tonal foundation behind heartfelt ballads, soulful solos, and emotionally gripping themes from *Tum Hi Ho* and *Kabhi Jo Badal Barse* to *Stairway to Heaven*.

In our [previous blog](/guitar-notes/guitar-intervals/), we looked at all 12 notes of the fretboard, and then extracted the bright [**Major Scale**](/guitar-notes/major-scales-guide/). 

Now, let's look at what happens when we tweak that recipe to create depth, suspense, and raw emotion.

---

## 1. The Indian Classical Connection (Sargam)

In Indian classical music, the **Natural Minor Scale** corresponds directly to **Asavari Thaat**:

* **Major Scale (Bilawal Thaat):** `Sa – Re – Ga – Ma – Pa – Dha – Ni – Sa` *(All Shuddha / Natural notes)*
* **Natural Minor Scale (Asavari Thaat):** `Sa – Re – komal Ga (g) – Ma – Pa – komal Dha (d) – komal Ni (n) – Sa`

Notice what changed: the **3rd (Ga)**, **6th (Dha)**, and **7th (Ni)** are all flattened (*Komal*), which strips away the bright optimism and replaces it with an emotional, grounded atmosphere.

---

## 2. The Minor Scale Recipe (W-H-W-W-H-W-W)

Every Natural Minor scale follows a strict step-by-step formula using **Whole Steps (W = 2 frets)** and **Half Steps (H = 1 fret)**:

<div class="table-responsive">

| Step | Type | Fret Movement | Target Note Degree | Comparison with Major |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---|:---|
| **Start** | Root | 0 frets | **1 (Root)** | Unchanged |
| **Step 1** | Whole (W) | +2 frets | **2 (Major 2nd)** | Unchanged |
| **Step 2** | Half (H) | +1 fret | **b3 (Minor 3rd)** | **Flattened by 1 fret** |
| **Step 3** | Whole (W) | +2 frets | **4 (Perfect 4th)** | Unchanged |
| **Step 4** | Whole (W) | +2 frets | **5 (Perfect 5th)** | Unchanged |
| **Step 5** | Half (H) | +1 fret | **b6 (Minor 6th)** | **Flattened by 1 fret** |
| **Step 6** | Whole (W) | +2 frets | **b7 (Minor 7th)** | **Flattened by 1 fret** |
| **Step 7** | Whole (W) | +2 frets | **8 / 1 (Octave Root)** | Unchanged |

</div>

> **The Universal Memory Rule:**
> **W – H – W – W – H – W – W**
> *(Whole – Half – Whole – Whole – Half – Whole – Whole)*

The crucial difference lies in the **Minor 3rd (b3)**: it sits just 3 frets above the root instead of 4, defining the signature sad and serious tone.

---

## 3. Cooking Two Examples

### Example A: A Natural Minor (The Pure Natural Scale)
Starting from Root **A**, applying **W-H-W-W-H-W-W**:
* **A** +(W)→ **B** +(H)→ **C** +(W)→ **D** +(W)→ **E** +(H)→ **F** +(W)→ **G** +(W)→ **A**
* **Notes:** `A - B - C - D - E - F - G - A` *(Zero sharps, zero flats! Relative minor to C Major)*

### Example B: E Natural Minor
Starting from Root **E**, applying **W-H-W-W-H-W-W**:
* **E** +(W)→ **F#** +(H)→ **G** +(W)→ **A** +(W)→ **B** +(H)→ **C** +(W)→ **D** +(W)→ **E**
* **Notes:** `E - F# - G - A - B - C - D - E` *(Contains one sharp: F#)*

## 4. Linear Single-String View (A Minor on the 5th String)

Let's trace the formula along the **5th string (A string)** starting from the open string or **A (12th fret)**, or from **A (5th fret on the 6th string)**:

* **5th Fret:** A (Root - 1)
* **7th Fret:** B (Major 2nd - 2) — *+2 frets (W)*
* **8th Fret:** C (Minor 3rd - b3) — *+1 fret (H)*
* **10th Fret:** D (Perfect 4th - 4) — *+2 frets (W)*
* **12th Fret:** E (Perfect 5th - 5) — *+2 frets (W)*
* **13th Fret:** F (Minor 6th - b6) — *+1 fret (H)*
* **15th Fret:** G (Minor 7th - b7) — *+2 frets (W)*
* **17th Fret:** A (Octave - 8) — *+2 frets (W)*

## 5. Folding into a Playable Fretboard Box (A Minor — Frets 5 to 8)

Sliding all the way to fret 17 is clumsy. By folding across the strings using our standard tuning intervals, we get the legendary **Position 1 Minor Scale Box** (the foundation of rock, blues, and fingerstyle solos):

{{< guitar title="A Natural Minor Scale (2-Octave Box Position — Frets 5 to 8)" frets="5,6,7,8" notes="6:5:A:root, 6:7:B, 6:8:C, 5:5:D, 5:7:E, 5:8:F, 4:5:G, 4:7:A:root, 3:4:B, 3:5:C, 3:7:D, 2:5:E, 2:6:F, 2:8:G, 1:5:A:root, 1:7:B, 1:8:C" >}}

### Step-by-Step String Route:
* **6th String (Low E):** Fret 5 (A - Root), Fret 7 (B), Fret 8 (C)
* **5th String (A):** Fret 5 (D), Fret 7 (E), Fret 8 (F)
* **4th String (D):** Fret 5 (G), Fret 7 (A - Octave 1)
* **3rd String (G):** Fret 4 (B), Fret 5 (C), Fret 7 (D)
* **2nd String (B):** Fret 5 (E), Fret 6 (F), Fret 8 (G)
* **1st String (High e):** Fret 5 (A - Octave 2), Fret 7 (B), Fret 8 (C)

## 7. Practice Tip:
Play the **Major Scale** and the **Minor Scale** back-to-back starting from the same root note (e.g., A Major vs. A Minor). Listen closely to how flattening just 3 notes (3rd, 6th, and 7th) completely transforms the mood of your guitar from a festival anthem to a deep, contemplative ballad!
